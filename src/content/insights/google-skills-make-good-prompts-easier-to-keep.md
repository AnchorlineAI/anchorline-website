A useful prompt has a short life when it lives only in one person's chat history.

Google's new Skills feature gives Gemini users a way to save instructions and reference files for work they repeat. Google says Skills are rolling out globally to Google AI subscription tiers, can be called by name, may run when a matching request appears, and can be combined. Support for Google Workspace is coming in the weeks ahead. Google also says personal Gems will be retired in November 2026 and Workspace versions later, with eligible Gems migrated automatically.

The feature is practical because a good method is often built through repetition. A team figures out the right source material, the questions to ask, the output format, and the checks that keep a draft from getting loose. Without a place to keep that method, the next person starts over or copies a prompt without knowing why it works.

A Skill can preserve instructions. It cannot decide whether those instructions are still sound, whether the reference files are current, or whether the output is ready to use. That still needs an owner, clear inputs, boundaries, and a review point. A saved prompt is not a governed operating system.

The better use is modest: capture one recurring piece of work, test it with real examples, and keep the underlying method somewhere the team can inspect. That is how a prompt becomes reusable without becoming invisible.
