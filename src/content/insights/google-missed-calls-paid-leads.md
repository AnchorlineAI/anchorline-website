For some Google campaigns, a missed call can now cost money.

Google's current documentation for Performance Max campaigns with pay-per-lead goals says a missed call can count as a valid lead when it happens during business hours and the caller stays on the line for more than 20 seconds. The company is moving Local Services Ads into this campaign type in phases. Its first U.S. rollout covers selected home and storefront services, not every Local Services Ads account or category.

The details are easy to miss. If a business uses a phone menu, the 20-second clock starts after the caller presses a key. Google uses the hours on the Business Profile, along with the active ad schedule, to decide when a missed call can qualify. A voicemail greeting during those hours is treated as a connected call. Once a lead is charged, follow-up calls or messages from the same phone number or email generally are not charged again for 15 days.

Trade coverage based on advertiser notices pointed to October 1. Google's public documentation describes a phased transition, though, rather than one effective date for every advertiser. Businesses should check their campaign type and current eligibility before assuming the rule applies to them.

This isn't automatically an argument for an AI receptionist. It is an argument for knowing what happens when the phone rings: who owns the call, what the listed hours say, whether someone can answer, how an inquiry is captured, and whether the lead became qualified work. A paid lead and a useful customer are not the same thing.
