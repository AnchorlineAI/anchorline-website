Searchable's [September 25 announcement](https://www.searchable.com/changelog/2026-09-25-prompt-universe) introduced Prompt Universe, a way to explore buyer questions before choosing which ones to track. The company says it maps possible questions around a business's products, audiences, competitors and buying situations, then recommends a smaller selection for monitoring.

That's a useful place to start. Before you use an AI visibility score to decide where to spend time or money, you need to know what went into it.

Imagine a roofing company that serves homeowners and commercial properties. A report built mostly around emergency home repairs could miss the facilities manager comparing contractors for a warehouse roof. Adding twenty variations of the home-repair question wouldn't fix that gap. You'd still be measuring the wrong mix of work.

The questions should reflect the customers and jobs you're trying to win. Include people who are learning about their options as well as those comparing providers. Look for missing services, locations and buying situations before adding more prompts that say roughly the same thing.

Searchable's [product explanation](https://www.searchable.com/prompt-universe) makes an important distinction: the map models what buyers could ask. It isn't a record of private AI conversations, and the size of the list doesn't tell you how many people asked those questions. Your tracking sample is a selection from that map, not a measure of demand.

Keep a consistent set of questions when comparing results over time. You can add questions as the business changes, but record those changes and compare the original set separately. Otherwise, an apparent improvement might come from changing the test.

Before deciding what [search visibility work](/services/search-visibility/) to do next, open a few of the underlying answers. See which businesses appeared, what sources were cited and which buyer needs the report left out.
