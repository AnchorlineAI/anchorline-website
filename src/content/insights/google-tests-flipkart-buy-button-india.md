Google is testing a way for some people in India to buy selected Flipkart products from Gemini and AI Mode. TechCrunch reports that a Buy button opens a Flipkart-branded checkout without sending the shopper away from the AI experience.

That is a narrow test, not a new rule for every retailer. The report says it is limited to some users and a small group of listings, including smartphones, electronics, and mobile accessories. Google told TechCrunch that it regularly tests new features and experiences, but did not provide more detail. Flipkart did not comment.

## What this does not tell us

It does not tell us who will be included later, how products will be ranked, what the commercial terms are, or whether the test changes conversion. It also does not establish a general rollout. TechCrunch reported that a person familiar with the matter expected a broader expansion in October; that is not the same as a public product announcement.

Still, the test makes one practical point clearer. A product can no longer depend only on the page a person reaches after a search. Before any checkout question can matter, the offer has to make sense where it is found and compared.

For a product seller, that means the basics need to hold together: the title, model or product ID, price, availability, variant, specifications, compatibility, shipping, returns, warranty, and the merchant behind the offer. A photo cannot stand in for a missing fact.

Service businesses have a version of the same job. What is the service? Who is it for? Where is it offered? What does pricing depend on? What proof, policy, or next step can a buyer actually see?

Clear information does not promise placement in an AI result or access to a checkout flow. It does make the business easier to understand when a person, a search engine, or an assistant reaches the offer.
