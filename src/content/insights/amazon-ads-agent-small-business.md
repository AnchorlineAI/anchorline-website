Amazon is bringing more of the work of planning and running ads into a single AI-powered platform. For a small business without a dedicated media team, that could make campaigns possible that previously took more time and expertise than the business had.

In its [September 29 announcement](https://advertising.amazon.com/library/news/amazon-ads-agent), Amazon describes Ads Agent as a way to connect campaign planning, execution and optimization. Advertisers supply business goals, budgets, products and creative direction. The platform takes on more of the work of building and adjusting the campaign.

The appeal is easy to understand. A small team might have a good product and a clear idea of its customers, yet struggle to turn that into a media plan. Help choosing where to advertise and managing the daily adjustments could free the owner to spend more time on the business itself.

## What businesses can use now

The launch has several parts with different availability. Amazon says Full-Funnel Campaigns is available to all U.S. advertisers, with creative approved before launch. DVA+ is in a limited U.S. beta, with an open beta planned for late October and broader international availability targeted for early 2027. Additional chat-based planning, audience and analytical features are due over the coming months.

An owner should check which capabilities are actually available in the account before building a campaign around them. Amazon also retains advanced settings for advertisers who want more direct control.

## Give it a budget you can explain

The business still needs to define what a worthwhile result would be. A campaign meant to reach new customers should be judged differently from one aimed at people already likely to buy.

Before letting the system run, agree on a spending ceiling and who can change it. Keep someone responsible for approving claims in the creative. Ask for an explanation when the platform shifts the budget, and compare its sales report with the business's own orders, margins and customer information.

Those checks can be proportionate to the campaign. A small test doesn't need a large approval committee. It does need someone who can tell whether additional sales are worth the money being spent, rather than assuming every attributed order was caused by the ad.

Amazon is offering smaller teams help with work that can be difficult to staff. A sensible first use would be one affordable campaign with a clear objective, reviewed against the business's own results before increasing the budget.
