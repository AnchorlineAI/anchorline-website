Search is no longer a single list of blue links. People still use traditional search engines, but they also ask direct questions, compare local options, and rely on generative systems to summarize complex subjects.

That shift has popularized three terms: SEO, AEO, and GEO. AEO and GEO can help describe how people encounter answers and AI-generated summaries. They are not established shortcuts around search fundamentals or reasons to run three disconnected programs. [Google's guidance for generative search](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide) treats optimization for its AI features as part of SEO.

## SEO supports discovery in search engines

Search engine optimization helps search engines crawl, understand, and evaluate a website. It includes technical foundations such as indexability, canonical URLs, and internal links, along with useful content that matches a real search need. Page experience and applicable structured data also have roles, but no special markup is required solely to appear in Google's generative search features.

Strong SEO makes the site understandable to both people and search systems. It does not guarantee a ranking, and it cannot compensate indefinitely for unclear positioning or weak evidence.

## AEO focuses on answer clarity

Answer engine optimization is a label for making information easy to find and understand when someone asks a question.

Clear headings, direct explanations, and logical page structure help readers and search systems understand a page. A useful answer should give the reader enough context to make a decision, not just repeat the question in different words. None of these practices guarantees that an answer engine will use the page.

## GEO names generative discovery

Generative engine optimization describes an interest in how AI-powered search and answer systems summarize or reference a business or subject. The term does not describe one uniform ranking system across providers.

Accessible pages, credible claims, clear relationships between concepts, and useful source material make expertise easier to understand and verify. They are sensible publishing practices, not a guaranteed recipe for a citation. Each provider decides what to retrieve and show.

## The shared foundation matters more than the labels

The labels overlap because the same page may serve a reader, a conventional search result, and an AI-assisted answer.

For example, a service page can say plainly whom the service is for, what the work includes, where it is offered, and what supports its claims. That helps a buyer evaluate the offer. It also gives search systems a clearer page to interpret without writing a separate version for every new search interface.

A technically crawlable page with vague content is weak. A thoughtful article hidden behind poor architecture is difficult to discover. Structured data cannot rescue unsupported claims. Publishing at volume does not create authority by itself.

A stronger foundation connects:

- clear positioning and service language;
- logical site architecture and internal links;
- technically accessible pages;
- useful answers to real customer questions;
- consistent organization, person, and service information;
- evidence that supports what the business says.

## Treat visibility as part of the growth system

Being found is only the beginning. The page a visitor reaches must help them understand the offer, build confidence, and take an appropriate next step. For local businesses, that may connect search visibility with reputation and lead capture. For B2B organizations, it may connect expertise with buyer research and a relevant conversation.

Anchorline treats search and answer visibility as capabilities inside the [Growth Engine](/growth-engine/), not as isolated promises. The work should support the same customer journey as the website, acquisition activity, conversion path, and follow-up.

The labels will change. The durable work is to publish information that people can use and verify, then measure whether the right visitors can find it and act on it.
