Google's [guidance on AI-generated website content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content), updated October 1, calls manual fact-checking before publication "critical." It also says that review applies to page titles, meta descriptions, structured data and image alt text.

That second part is easy to miss. You can read an article carefully and still publish a description that overstates what it says. Structured data, the information a page gives search engines in a machine-readable format, can contain the wrong author or date even when the page looks fine.

AI can help with this work. Google's guidance still recognizes its usefulness for research and organizing original content. The same page warns that generating lots of pages without adding value may violate its scaled-content-abuse policy. That's different from saying AI-assisted articles are automatically a problem.

For a business using AI to publish, I'd make the review concrete. Open the sources behind the important claims. Check whether a number is a company's estimate or an independently measured result. Read the description alongside the finished article, and make sure the title doesn't promise more than the piece delivers.

Then check the less visible details. Does the author information match? Is the publication date right? Does the image description say what's actually in the photo? Validate the structured data, and inspect the page on a phone before the person responsible approves it.

Those checks belong in [search visibility work](/services/search-visibility/) alongside writing something worth reading. This documentation update isn't an announcement of a new ranking factor, and passing a checklist doesn't guarantee a search position. It gives the person publishing a much better chance of catching a mistake before readers do.
