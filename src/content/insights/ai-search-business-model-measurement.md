Google is getting more searches. Making money from those searches is still changing.

In its [third-quarter results](https://s206.q4cdn.com/479360582/files/doc_news/2026/Oct/28/attachments/2026q3-alphabet-earnings-release.pdf), Alphabet reported its strongest Search query growth in five years. Search and other advertising revenue rose 12% to $63.35 billion. But on the [earnings call](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/googl/transcripts/712854-q3-2026/), the company said AI Mode was earning less from its queries and that its growth affected the results.

That isn't a story about Google falling apart. Currency changes were the biggest reason Search revenue growth slowed from the previous quarter; retail and media also played a part. Google says its ad experiments are promising and it's expanding them. It hasn't disclosed the size of AI Mode's monetization gap.

For a business owner, there's a practical question here: when AI helps someone discover your business, what happens next?

Someone might read an answer, ask follow-up questions and make a decision before visiting your website. Counting mentions won't tell you whether those people understood your offer or became customers.

I'd start by checking whether AI describes your business accurately. Then look for useful actions: visits from the right people, calls, inquiries, bookings and purchases. Connect those to actual revenue where the evidence allows it. Where the connection isn't clear, say so.

That's the approach I'd take at Anchorline: follow visibility through to a useful action and a business result. It's a measurement recommendation, not a promise that every step can be tracked.

Keep making your business easier to find and understand. Just don't let a growing mention count become the whole definition of progress.
