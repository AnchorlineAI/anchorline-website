Publishers have found a new service to sell: helping brands show up in AI answers.

[Digiday reports](https://digiday.com/media/media-briefing-publishers-are-turning-geo-from-an-experiment-into-a-business/) that Future has sold its GEO product to more than 30 clients, while Ziff Davis says it has several clients of its own. The work includes content refreshes, branded content, and efforts to improve visibility or citations in AI-generated answers. The service grows out of the same [SEO, AEO, and GEO](/insights/seo-aeo-and-geo-what-they-are-and-why-they-need-to-work-together/) conversation happening on many company sites. That tells us there is demand. It does not yet tell us what the work returns.

An AI citation can be useful evidence that a page is understandable and present in the conversation. It isn't proof that a buyer visited the site, took action, became a qualified lead, or produced revenue. Those are different events, and the path between them is still hard to measure reliably.

That gap matters because GEO can easily become a report full of mentions with no clear commercial meaning. A brand should ask what was changed, where it was seen, what the measurement can and cannot show, and whether the work improved the source material on the company's own site. The answer may be worthwhile before an AI platform ever cites it.

There is also a disclosure question. When a publisher is paid to create or refresh material intended to influence AI visibility, readers and clients should be able to see what is sponsored, what is editorial, and what is simply an experiment.
